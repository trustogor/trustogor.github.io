The Problem
GitLab's backend engineering teams were running their own hiring with zero coordination. 10 teams, 10 different interview processes, no shared rubric, no data. There was a 6-month engineering backlog directly stalling product roadmap execution.
6 months
Hiring backlog blocking roadmap execution
No Process Consistency
Each team ran their own interview loop. Loops ranged from 2–5+ rounds with no standardization.
Weak Alignment
Hiring managers disagreed on what "good" looked like. No shared success profile or evaluation rubric.
Slow Decisions
Candidates waited 2–3 weeks between rounds. Debriefs were ad-hoc. No clear decision timeline.
Zero Visibility
Leadership couldn't see funnel health, bottlenecks, or where delays were actually happening.
The Solution
I rebuilt the hiring process from first principles as a unified system across all 10 teams
01. Intake
Structured role calibration template. Define success upfront. Align all stakeholders before opening.
02. Interviews
Right-sized loops. Clear ownership at each stage. 48-hr feedback SLAs. Consistent format across all roles.
03. Training
Trained all interviewers on structured format. Role-specific scorecards. Calibration before each round.
04. Data
Weekly hiring dashboard. Stage conversion tracking. Time-in-stage visibility. Bottleneck escalation.
05. Candidate Experience
Standardised communication SLAs. High-touch engagement. Strong offer narratives. Market positioning.
06. Leadership
Weekly hiring standup with Director of Engineering and Engineering Managers to review pipeline health, address bottlenecks, and drive timely hiring decisions.

Philosophy
Thinking

How I Think
About Talent.

These are not frameworks I read about. They are conclusions I reached by doing the work, making mistakes, and paying close attention to what actually moved the needle.

01
Hiring is a system, not a series of transactions.
Every role you fill should make the next one easier. That only happens when you build infrastructure — pipelines, process, data, playbooks — that compounds over time. I don’t just fill seats. I build the engine that keeps filling them.
02
Technology should augment the human parts, not replace them.
AI and automation should make recruiters faster, sharper, and better informed — not remove them from the equation. The empathy, the read on a candidate, the relationship with a hiring manager — those stay human. Always.
03
Data tells you where to go. Judgment gets you there.
I run experiments. I track signal. I double down on what works and cut what doesn’t. But data doesn’t make the final call — it informs it. Knowing how to read a number and knowing what it means for a human being are two different skills.
04
Stakeholder trust is the job.
A talent function is only as strong as its relationship with the people it serves. If they don’t trust the process, they bypass it. My job is to earn that trust early, hold the standard when it’s uncomfortable, and make hiring feel like a strategic advantage.
