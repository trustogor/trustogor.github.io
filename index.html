Case studies

Three problems, three different shapes of hiring.

Process design — GitLab, backend & infrastructure engineering

Unifying ten teams' worth of ad-hoc hiring into one system

GitLab's backend engineering teams were each running their own hiring with zero coordination: ten teams, ten different interview processes, no shared rubric, no data. A six-month engineering backlog was directly stalling roadmap execution. I rebuilt the process from first principles as a unified system across all ten teams: structured intake to calibrate success upfront, right-sized interview loops with 48-hour feedback SLAs, and training for every interviewer on a consistent, scorecard-based format.

Backlog cleared in 3 months Team grew 16→34 (113%) 95% offer acceptance 90% hired from sourcing

Commended by the VP of Engineering for delivery speed and quality, operating fully remote-first across global time zones.

0→1 build — On Deck, first recruiter

Building the entire hiring engine from scratch

On Deck was scaling fast with no recruiting infrastructure. Hiring was reactive and random: no sourcing strategy, no outreach playbook, no interview framework, and response rates so low the pipeline was constantly dry. As the first recruiter, I built and scaled the end-to-end hiring process across Engineering, Product, Design, and ML, introducing a sourcing strategy, structured interview cadence, funnel reporting, and a personalized video outreach approach using early AI tools that transformed pipeline conversion.

Time-to-hire 60→30 days 31 hires in 9 months 100% offer acceptance +30% diversity representation

85% of hires came from sourcing, the clearest evidence the outreach strategy itself was the lever, not just headcount thrown at the problem.

Founder-level operations — LocalLinked, MBA capstone

Running GTM, hiring, and revenue for a four-person founding team

LocalLinked, a student discount and rewards platform, was preparing to launch with no route to students, no local business network, no revenue model, and no hiring plan. Embedded with two founders, I led the launch architecture across every function simultaneously: secured an exclusive university partnership for direct campus access, signed 36 local businesses on a commission-only model, designed the end-to-end revenue framework, and built the hiring blueprint for founding roles including a Student Ambassador Programme.

780 users onboarded week one 78% of 1,000-user target 36 businesses signed pre-launch

Hit 2,000 active users within 8 weeks of launch, demonstrating founder-level operational capability across GTM, hiring, and product strategy under real startup constraints.

AI-augmented hiring

Technology should augment the human parts, not replace them.

AI and automation should make recruiters faster, sharper, and better informed, not remove them from the equation. The empathy, the read on a candidate, the relationship with a hiring manager, those stay human. Always.

Interview orchestration

Calendar syncing, automatic prep packs, live note-taking, and auto-generated summaries, so candidates feel prepared and interviewers have structured notes instantly.

Candidate experience

Automated journeys that feel human: prep materials, culture deep-dives, and team bios personalized to the candidate and role, sent at the right moment.

Hiring intelligence

Weekly dashboards turning scattered ATS data into decision-ready intelligence: funnel health, bottlenecks, source performance, candidate themes.

Talent mapping at scale

AI-powered mapping across geographies and markets: talent clusters, adjacent title and company mapping, skill trajectory analysis. Patterns most recruiters miss.

Point of view

What I believe about this work.

Data tells you where to go. Judgment gets you there.

I run experiments, track signal, double down on what works and cut what doesn't. But data doesn't make the final call, it informs it. Knowing how to read a number and knowing what it means for a human being are two different skills.

Stakeholder trust is the job.

A talent function is only as strong as its relationship with the people it serves. If they don't trust the process, they bypass it. My job is to earn that trust early, hold the standard when it's uncomfortable, and make hiring feel like a strategic advantage.
